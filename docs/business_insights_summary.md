# Business Insights Summary

## Problem Statement
This project analyzes 4 years (2014-2017) of Superstore sales data to identify the key drivers of profitability, evaluate the impact of discounting and shipping practices, and recommend data-backed actions to improve margins and customer retention.

## Tools Used
Excel (pivot tables, Goal Seek sensitivity modeling), Python (pandas, matplotlib), SQL (MySQL — window functions, CTEs), Tableau (interactive dashboard)

## Core Findings

1. **Regional discounting is strongly linked to profitability, but not uniformly.** The West region has the lowest average discount (10.9%) and highest total profit ($108,419), while Central has the highest average discount (24.0%) and lowest total profit ($39,706).

2. **Some sub-categories can't absorb discounting the way others can.** Tables (26.1% avg discount) and Bookcases (21.1% avg discount) are the only two sub-categories operating at a net loss (-$17,726 and -$3,473 respectively), while Binders sustains a 37.2% average discount and still generates $30,222 in profit — proving discount level alone doesn't predict loss; underlying margin structure matters more.

3. **A 10% increase in freight costs would erase 80% of current profit** unless prices rise by at least 11.32% to compensate (Excel Goal Seek model); a 15% freight increase would push the business into a net loss of -$58,183 without a 17.09% price adjustment.

4. **Revenue shows strong, consistent Q4 seasonality and year-over-year growth.** Every year (2014-2017) shows a clear November/December spike, with peak monthly revenue growing from ~$64K (late 2014) to ~$118K (Nov 2017).

5. **New customer acquisition has declined sharply since 2014**, while retention of existing customers remains comparatively strong. New customers per cohort year: 595 (2014) → 136 (2015) → 51 (2016) → 11 (2017 partial). Meanwhile, the original 2014 cohort still had 517 active customers in 2017.

6. **Shipping speed shows only a mild, non-linear relationship with profit margin.** Fast shipping (0-3 days) has the highest average margin (13.77%), but nearly half of all orders (4,943 of 9,994) ship in the 4-5 day "Medium" window, which has the lowest average margin (10.71%) — suggesting shipping speed alone isn't a strong profit lever, though the volume concentration in that window merits operational review.

## Recommendations

1. **Cap discounts on Tables and Bookcases at 15%**, in line with what other profitable sub-categories sustain, to eliminate the -$21,198 combined annual loss currently generated by these two categories.

2. **Prepare for freight cost increases proactively.** If freight rises even 5%, a modest 5.70% price adjustment fully preserves margin — implement a freight-cost monitoring trigger tied to this pricing response.

3. **Increase inventory and staffing ahead of Q4** (starting September) to meet the consistent, multi-year holiday demand spike, rather than reacting to it in November/December.

4. **Investigate the decline in new customer acquisition.** Since existing customer retention is strong, marketing/acquisition spend — not retention programs — appears to be the higher-leverage area to address the shrinking pipeline of new customers.
